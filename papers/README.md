# Downloaded Papers

All PDFs were opened with `pypdf` and passed page-count validation. The four papers named in the research specification were split into three-page chunks under `papers/pages/`; every chunk was text-extracted and reviewed. Extracted text is retained in `artifacts/fulltexts/` for searchable handoff notes.

1. **Linear Probing Provides Robust and Efficient Detection of Machine-Generated Text** — Gerrit Quaremba, Hanqi Yan, Elizabeth Black, Denny Vrandečić, Elena Simperl (2026, EMNLP). `2608.24780_linear_probing_mgt.pdf`, 24 pp. Direct readout evidence for a shared continuous machine-text direction; code in `code/mgt_probes/`.
2. **SV-Detect: AI-generated Text Detection with Steering Vectors** — Mikhail Vishnyakov, Tatiana Gaintseva (2026, EMNLP). `2606.07313_sv_detect.pdf`, 27 pp. Layer-wise human/AI logistic directions used as detector features; explicitly does not intervene during generation. Code in `code/sv-detect/`.
3. **Feature-Level Insights into Artificial Text Detection with Sparse Autoencoders** — Kristian Kuznetsov et al. (2025). `2503.03601_sae_artificial_text_detection.pdf`, 22 pp. Gemma-2 residual-stream SAE features link detection to formality, repetition, complexity, and other interpretable cues; includes feature steering.
4. **The Assistant Axis: Situating and Stabilizing the Default Persona of Language Models** — Christina Lu, Jack Gallagher, Jonathan Michala, Kyle Fish, Jack Lindsey (2026). `2601.10387_assistant_axis.pdf`, 54 pp. Defines a causal assistant-persona direction and activation capping; essential confound/control. Code in `code/assistant-axis/`.
5. **Inspection and Control of Self-Generated-Text Recognition Ability in Llama3-8b-Instruct** — Christopher Ackerman, Nina Panickssery (ICLR 2025). `2410.02064_self_generated_text_recognition.pdf`, 32 pp. Closest causal precedent: a residual-stream vector controls authorship judgments and perception, but does not test whether free generations become stylistically more AI-like.
6. **Steering Language Models With Activation Engineering** — Alexander Matt Turner et al. (2023/2024). `2308.10248_activation_engineering.pdf`, 32 pp. Establishes contrastive activation addition and preservation of off-target behavior.
7. **Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency** — Andy Zou et al. (2023, revised 2025). `2310.01405_representation_engineering.pdf`, 56 pp. General framework and reusable control pipeline for population-level directions. Code in `code/representation-engineering/`.
8. **The Effectiveness of Style Vectors for Steering Large Language Models: A Human Evaluation** — Diaoulé Diallo et al. (2026). `2601.21505_style_vectors_human_eval.pdf`, 16 pp. Human evidence that moderate steering can change perceived tone while retaining quality.
9. **RAID: A Shared Benchmark for Robust Evaluation of Machine-Generated Text Detectors** — Liam Dugan et al. (ACL 2024). `2405.07940_raid.pdf`, 30 pp. Robust detector benchmark across generators, domains, decoding, and attacks. Code in `code/raid/`.
10. **How Close is ChatGPT to Human Experts? Comparison Corpus, Evaluation, and Detection** — Biyang Guo et al. (2023). `2301.07597_hc3.pdf`, 20 pp. Introduces HC3 and detector/linguistic baselines. Code in `code/chatgpt-comparison-detection/`.
11. **Do LLMs Write Like Humans? Variation in Grammatical and Rhetorical Styles** — Alex Reinhart et al. (PNAS 2025). `2410.16107_hape_style_variation.pdf`, 7 pp. Introduces HAP-E and shows systematic rhetorical differences, especially after instruction tuning.
12. **MAGE: Machine-generated Text Detection in the Wild** — Yafu Li et al. (ACL 2024). `2305.13242_mage.pdf`, 18 pp. Broad in-the-wild detection dataset and OOD evaluation.

Direct sources are the corresponding arXiv abstract/PDF pages; arXiv identifiers are embedded in filenames.
